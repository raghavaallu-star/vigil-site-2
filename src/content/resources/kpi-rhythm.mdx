Most plants don't lack data or dashboards. What they lack is a reliable habit of looking at the right numbers, at the right level, often enough to act. A governance rhythm provides that habit.

Here's the three-tier cadence we build with plants in [Plant Performance Governance](/product/governance).

## Daily: the 15-minute stand-up

**Who:** shift leads, production, maintenance and process representatives.

**What to review:**

- Yesterday's production: kiln output, clinker and cement tonnes.
- Stops and their causes, both planned and unplanned.
- Open alerts from the predictive models, and who is checking each one.
- Today's priorities and any safety concerns.

**Rules:** stand up, stick to fifteen minutes, and give every issue an owner before you leave. Deeper discussion moves to a separate huddle.

## Weekly: the cross-functional review

**Who:** heads of production, maintenance, process and quality.

**What to review:**

- **Trends, not single days:** run factor, MTBF on critical assets, specific heat (kcal/kg clinker), specific power (kWh/t cement).
- **Bad actors:** the top repeat failures this week, and the status of their root cause analyses.
- **Maintenance health:** planned-work percentage, PM compliance, schedule compliance and backlog.
- **Energy losses:** false-air and grinding efficiency flags.
- **Open actions** from previous weeks, with any that are overdue.

The weekly meeting is where cross-functional problems get solved, because they touch more than one department.

## Monthly: the leadership review

**Who:** plant head and functional leaders.

**What to review:**

- KPI trends against the baseline and targets in all four families: production, reliability, energy and maintenance.
- **Benefit realization:** what has actually improved against the measured baseline, and what hasn't yet.
- The improvement roadmap, including resourcing and what moves next.

## Four KPI families

Keep the list short. These four families cover most of what matters:

| Family | Example KPIs |
| --- | --- |
| Production | Kiln output, run factor, clinker and cement tonnes |
| Reliability | MTBF, unplanned downtime, repeat failures |
| Energy | kcal/kg clinker, kWh/t cement |
| Maintenance | Planned work %, PM compliance, schedule compliance, backlog |

## What makes it stick

- **One source of numbers.** If people argue about whose data is right, the meeting fails. A [historian](/product/historian) ends that argument.
- **Every action has an owner and a date.** Actions stay visible until they are closed.
- **Same time, same place, same format.** Consistency beats cleverness.
- **Leaders show up.** If the monthly review is skipped, the weekly one will be too.

Start with the daily stand-up on one line. Once it runs without effort, add the weekly review. The rhythm becomes the plant's operating system, and every other improvement depends on it.
