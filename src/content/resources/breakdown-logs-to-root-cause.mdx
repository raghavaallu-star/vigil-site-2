Ask a reliability engineer why a raw mill gearbox failed last quarter and you'll often get a good answer. Ask for the evidence behind it and you'll get a notebook entry, a work order with "bearing replaced" in the close-out field, and a promise to pull the trends "when there's time".

The knowledge exists. It just isn't connected.

## Two halves of the same story

Every significant breakdown has two records:

- **The field record:** what the engineer saw, heard and did, including the failure mode, the parts replaced and the time it went down and came back.
- **The process record:** what the sensors saw in the days and hours before, such as vibration, temperatures, current and load.

Root cause analysis needs both, side by side, on one timeline. In most plants they sit in different systems, owned by different teams, and only meet during a formal RCA, if at all.

## Capture the field record properly

The first step is making breakdown logging fast and structured enough that it actually happens. In the [Engineer Mobile App](/product/engineer-app):

- The engineer picks the asset from the same hierarchy the historian uses.
- They choose a failure mode from a short, standard list, and can add a note and a photo.
- Start and end times are captured automatically.

It takes under a minute. More importantly, every log is now tied to an asset ID and a timestamp that the process data understands.

## Link it automatically

Once a breakdown is logged, the [Root Cause Agent](/agents/root-cause) assembles a single timeline:

1. Historian trends for the asset in the days before the event.
2. Any AI alerts or health-score changes that fired.
3. Recent inspections and check results from the app.
4. The work-order history for the asset.

It then ranks which signals changed most before the failure and looks for similar past events on the same asset class.

## Close the loop with predictions

This is where the value compounds. For every breakdown, you can now answer:

- **Was it predicted?** Did an alert fire, and how far ahead?
- **Was it acted on?** If an alert fired, what happened next?
- **Is it a repeat?** Has this failure mode occurred on this asset before?

Predicted-but-missed events point to process gaps. Unpredicted events point to models that need new signals or more training data. Repeats go straight onto the **bad-actor list** in your [Reliability Excellence](/product/reliability-excellence) program.

## What engineers still do

None of this replaces engineering judgment. The agent drafts a starting point and the reliability team confirms, corrects and decides on corrective actions. What changes is the starting point. Instead of a blank page and an afternoon of spreadsheet work, the engineer starts with a timeline that already has the evidence on it.

## Start small

You don't need every asset connected to begin. Pick one line, log every breakdown in the app for a quarter, and review the linked timelines in the weekly meeting. Within a few months the patterns, and the repeat offenders, become hard to ignore.
